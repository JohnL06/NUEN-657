Aerial Measuring System — [[DOE]]'s airborne radiation detection capability, flying fixed-wing aircraft and helicopters to map contamination from the air.

- Covers ground [[Deposition amount]] far faster than ground [[Field Teams]] can walk it
- One of the [[NEST]] programs
- Written in the 09/16 lecture as "Aerial Measurement System"; DOE's name for it is Aerial Measuring System

Fixed wing, the old plane — Beechcraft King Air B-200:
- Twin-engine turboprop, IFR (all weather) rated
- 260 knots (300 mph), range 1,130 nm (1,300 sm), max endurance 5 hrs without refueling
- Four person crew: pilot, co-pilot, scientist/health physicist, technician/technologist
- Carries [[SPARCS]]

Fixed wing, the new plane — Beechcraft King Air 350ER:
- Three built by Textron Aviation and unveiled 18 December 2019, replacing the aging B-200s to improve AMS reliability and range
- Based at the Remote Sensing Laboratory: two at Joint Base Andrews, MD and one at Nellis AFB, NV
- 303 knots max cruise, 1,878 nm range, 35,000 ft ceiling, 16,500 lb max takeoff weight — ER is the extended-range variant
- Roughly 10 hrs endurance, about 7-8 hrs with the detection hardware fitted, so it can self-deploy anywhere in the continental US
- Carries specialized radiation detection systems measuring both air and ground contamination
- Also flown for security at major public events: presidential inaugurations, the Super Bowl, the Boston Marathon
- Why it replaced the B-200: faster (303 vs 260 kt), two-thirds more range (1,878 vs 1,130 nm), and roughly double the endurance (~10 vs 5 hrs)

Rotary wing — Bell 412, "lower and slower":
- Twin-Pac turboshaft engine, IFR rated
- 120 knots (140 mph), range 360 nm (410 sm), max endurance 3 hrs without refueling
- Three person crew: pilot, co-pilot, technician/technologist
- Ground support: scientist/health physicist, data analyst, aircraft mechanic
- NNSA's current rotary-wing fleet is reported as two Leonardo AW139s rather than the Bell 412 — single source, worth checking against the slides

Mission parameters:
- On-call response phase, large area (>25 mi2) — 500-1,000 ft altitude, 1-5 mile spacing, 140 knots, fixed wing
- Radiological mapping, rapid overview for emergency response — 500 ft, 0.5 mile spacing, 80 knots, helicopter
- Radiological mapping, small area (<25 mi2) detailed survey — 150-300 ft, 250-500 ft line spacing, 70 knots
