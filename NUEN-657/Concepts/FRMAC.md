#concept #center
Federal Radiological Monitoring and Assessment Center — the interagency center that consolidates all federal radiological monitoring and assessment during a response, so there is one set of numbers and one voice.

- Managed and organized by [[DOE]], whichever agency holds [[Primary Authority]] for the incident itself
- Established because of the disorganization at [[TMI]], where multiple agencies produced competing data and messaging
- Control transfers from DOE to [[EPA]] only after the emergency is stabilized, offsite releases have stopped, offsite conditions have been evaluated, an initial long-range monitoring plan has been developed with [[SLTT]] and other federal agencies, and EPA has assurance of support
- Monitoring and assessment terminate when there is no further threat to public safety or the environment, SLTT capabilities are adequate, and all parties agree
- Should always be supporting the state response, never the other way around
- The [[CMRT]] deploys as its operational nucleus; see [[FRMAC manual]] for the dose assessment methods
- Mission: provide additional technical expertise and assistance to support local responders after a radiological release or accident — HP, atmospheric modeling, aerial measurements, environmental monitoring and sampling, data visualization, sample control and management
- Does: provide radiological monitoring, assess and evaluate monitoring results, give technical and tactical support to local organizations, manage all off-site monitoring data, and produce assessment reports and maps for protective action decisions
- Does NOT issue recommendations — that is the [[A-TEAM]]'s job
- Coordinates fixed off-site labs and on-site mobile labs, including the [[FAL]], plus support for medical providers

Initial response elements, in order of arrival:
- [[CMHT]] — scalable, near-immediate remote technical support
- [[CMAC]] — 6-7 person deployed team for early planning, leadership, and logistics
- [[CMRT]] — full field support, 50-100 people

Working with FRMAC:
- Send data via [[RadResponder]], or email/fax/bridge line to the CMHT
- Products are typically maps and spreadsheets (ArcGIS): AMS exposure rate and isotopic deposition maps; evacuation/shelter, relocation, potassium iodide, and worker stay-time maps; agricultural and dairy/beef impacts; monitoring status and planning maps
- The state/local/tribal jurisdiction does NOT pay — costs are generally absorbed by [[DHS]]
- Sends a liaison and/or scientific advisor to each [[EOC]]
- Needs 10,000-20,000 sq ft of indoor space near highways and airports, with power and parking for mobile labs — schools, hockey rinks, farm centers, armories
