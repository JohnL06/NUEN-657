#concept #role
The individual responsible for on-scene incident activities, including developing incident objectives and ordering and releasing resources. Has overall authority and responsibility for conducting incident operations. (IS-100.c glossary)

- The only ICS position that is always staffed — on a small incident, one person may accomplish all management functions
- Specifically responsible for: ensuring overall incident safety, providing information to internal/external stakeholders, and establishing/maintaining liaison with other agencies
- May appoint one or more Deputies, who must be fully qualified to assume the IC position if needed
- Qualifications should rest on training, experience, and expertise — not solely on rank, grade, or technical knowledge
- In a Unified Command there is no single "Commander" — agencies instead manage the incident through jointly approved objectives

Lecture context: as a responding scientist, our job is to advise the decision maker/Incident Commander (often the governor; the county judge in Texas) about [[evacuation]], [[relocation]], and [[shelter in place]] options. As contractors rather than federal employees, we typically can't tell the IC what to do outright — only point to the guidelines (see [[EPA400]]).
