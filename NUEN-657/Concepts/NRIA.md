#concept #policy
Nuclear/Radiological Incident Annex to the [[FIOP]] (referred to in lecture as the "Response and Recovery Federal Interagency Operational Manual").

- Addresses response and short-term recovery activities for incidents involving release of radioactive material
- Describes policies, situations, concepts of operations, and responsibilities of federal departments and agencies in such events
- Purpose:
	- Defines roles and responsibilities of federal agencies responding to nuclear/radiological events
	- Discusses authorities, capabilities, and assets the federal government has for responding to nuclear/radiological incidents, not otherwise specified in the [[NRF]]
	- Discusses integration of the Concept of Operations (CONOP) with other elements of the NRF
	- Provides guidelines for notification, coordination, and leadership of federal activities
- Focuses on activities that protect the public (health, safety, property, critical infrastructure, environment), restore government services, and provide emergency relief to government, businesses, and individuals affected
- Introduces key terms: [[LFA|Lead Federal Agency]] with [[Primary Authority]]; Cooperating Agency (agencies providing additional technical/resource support to the Department of Homeland Security and coordinating agencies); D/As (Department/Agencies); SLTT (State, Local, Tribal, and Territorial)
- Scope covers inadvertent releases (commercial/weapons production facilities, lost sources, transport accidents, domestic nuclear weapons incidents, foreign accidents affecting the US) and deliberate acts (RDDs, INDs, nuclear weapons)
- Most common incidents are loss, theft, or mismanagement of relatively small radioactive sources, typically handled at the local level
- The Accident Response Group (ARG) can scale to hundreds of people; once in the field, subgroups organize by [[ICS]]
- Sets administrative limits for the response itself: 10 rem for protection of property; emergency dose rate guidance of 10 rem over 48 hours within a 96-hour emergency window (12-hour workdays)
- Personnel benefit from alarming [[PRDs]] for continuous monitoring
