#concept #protectiveaction
Stable iodine (usually as KI) taken to saturate the thyroid so it does not take up radioiodine.

- Still reduces the dose if taken as late as about 2 hours after exposure
- Protects only against [[I-131]] and other radioiodines — nothing for other nuclides or external dose
- Use is covered by [[FDA]] guidance, alongside Prussian blue for cesium
- PAG: 5 rem projected child thyroid dose (EPA-400); the one-year-old age group gets the largest thyroid dose
- FDA thresholds (projected thyroid dose): ages 0-18 ≥5 rem; adults 18-40 and pregnant/lactating women ≥10 rem; adults over 40 ≥500 rem
- Doses: adults 130 mg; ages 3-18 65 mg (adolescents >150 lb get 130 mg); 1 month-3 years 32 mg; birth-1 month 16 mg
- Most effective before exposure; may require approval of state medical officials
- Lecture warned shellfish-allergic people against KI — but EPA-400 (2017) p.21 says iodine and shellfish allergies are NOT related
