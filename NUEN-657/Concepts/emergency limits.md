Dose limits applied specifically during a radiological emergency response, distinct from the standard 5 rem/year occupational dose limit used in routine operations.

- Used to keep public and emergency worker doses below the thresholds for deterministic health effects (deaths and injuries) during the acute response phase
- The first objective of radiological emergency response; only after this is satisfied does the focus shift to reducing stochastic risk (cancer, hereditary effects) per [[EPA400]] and the [[FRMAC manual]]
- EPA-400 emergency worker guidelines ("5-10-25"): 5 rem all occupational exposures; 10 rem protecting critical infrastructure; 25 rem lifesaving or protection of large populations; >25 rem only for people fully aware of the risks
- NCRP decision dose 50 rad; IAEA turn-back guidance 50 rem (prevent severe effects) and 100 rem (lifesaving)
- Zones: cold zone ≤10 mR/h; hot zone >10 mR/h; dangerous-radiation zone ≥10 R/h; turn back at 200 R/h even on lifesaving missions
- Also follow OSHA 29 CFR, EPA 40 CFR, NRC 10 CFR 20, DOE 10 CFR 835
