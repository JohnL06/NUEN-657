			- History of national response
				- 1979 First comprehensive plan for federal emergency response: [[FEMA]]
					- Federal Emergency Management existed in one form or another since ~1803
					- Between 1803-1979 piecemeal approach to disaster relief
					- FEMA first charged with absorbing Emergency Response duties from multiple agencies with disjointed plans
				- 1988
					- Authorized Director of FEMA to prepare Federal Response Plan ([[FRP]])
					- Established a system of federal assistance to state and local governments and required all states to prepare individual Emergency Operations Plans
				- 1992 FRP
					- Augmented by 1968 National Contingency Plan ([[NCP]]) originally intended for oil spills
					- scope expanded by 1980 comprehensive [[Environmental Response, Compensation and Liability Act]]
					- included emergency hazardous waste removal and facility specific contingency plans
				- 1996 Federal Radiological Emergency Response plan ([[FRERP]]) signed
					- Interagency document
						- NRC and FEMA jointly develop government response for peacetime radiological emergencies within US
					- Nuclear installations
					- Transportation incidents
					- nuclear weapon incidents
					- earth bound return satellites
					- Terrorist attacks
				- ?????
				- 2003 Homeland Security Presidential Directive 5 ([[HPSD-5]]) directs the development of the National Incident Management System ([[NIMS]])
					- NIMS adopted throughout all federal agencies
					- Emergency management doctrine that coordinates emergency preparedness and incident management among public and private sectors
					- Provides the template for the management of the incident (how the event should be organized)
				- 2004 National Response Plan ([[NRP]])
					- Provides the structure and mechanisms for national level policy for incident management
					- Addresses natural disasters and terrorist attacks
					- Updated after Hurricanes Katrina, Wilma, and Rita in 2005
				- 2008 National Response Framework ([[NRF]]) (2013, 2019 latest revision)
					- Part of National Strategy for homeland security and built on NIMS and [[ICS]]
					- Guiding principles for enabling all levels of domestic response to prepare for and provide a unified national response to disasters and emergencies (ranging from acts of God to terrorist activities)
					- Annexes developed and implemented to address specific events (biological, chemical, nuclear/radiological, etc.)

			- NRF response vision
				- NRF sets the strategy and doctrine for how the whole community builds, sustains, and delivers the response core capabilities identified in the National Preparedness Goal ([[NPG]]) in an integrated manner with the other mission areas. (Documents are available in Canvas)
				- NPG: "A secure and resilient nation with the capabilities required across the whole community to prevent, protect against, mitigate, respond to, and recover from the threats and hazards that pose the greatest risk."
					- 5 mission areas:
						- Prevention (avoid / stop threat)
						- Protection (protect public and assets against threats)
						- Mitigation (lessen the impact of future disasters)
						- Response (respond quickly and effectively)
						- Recovery (timely restoration)
					- 32 core capabilities needed to support missions

			- National Response Framework is comprised of
				- Core Document (51 pages!!)
				- Emergency Support Function ([[ESF]]) Annexes – describe the federal coordinating structures that group resources and capabilities into functional areas most frequently needed in a national response
				- Support Annexes – describe other mechanisms by which support is organized among private sector, NGO, and federal partners. The support annexes describe the essential supporting processes and considerations common to most incidents. Content found within the support annexes is superseded by changes and updates to legislation.
				- Previously had "Incident Annexes" – These are now under the Federal Interagency Operational Plan ([[FIOP]]) incident annexes (link in Canvas)
					- address the unique aspects of how we respond to seven broad incident categories (e.g., biological, nuclear/radiological, cyber, mass evacuation, etc.)
				- Two other annexes have been removed and are now in [[ESF 14]]
