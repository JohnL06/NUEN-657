NRF response vision
	- NRF sets the strategy and doctrine for how the whole community builds, sustains, and delivers the response core capabilities identified in the National Preparedness Goal ([[NPG]]) in an integrated manner with the other mission areas. (Documents are available in Canvas)
	- NPG: "A secure and resilient nation with the capabilities required across the whole community to prevent, protect against, mitigate, respond to, and recover from the threats and hazards that pose the greatest risk."
		- 5 mission areas:
			- Prevention (avoid / stop threat)
			- Protection (protect public and assets against threats)
			- Mitigation (lessen the impact of future disasters)
			- Response (respond quickly and effectively)
				- Recovery (timely restoration)
			- 32 core capabilities needed to support missions

Nuclear/Radiological Incident Annex ([[NRIA]])
	- Addresses k  edkater\ response and short term recovery activities for incidents involving release of radioactive materials
	- Describes policies situations concepts of operations and responsibilities of federal departments and agencies in such events
	- purposes: 
		- Define roles and responsibilities of Federal agencies responding to nuke/rad events
		- discuss specific authorities, capabilities, and assets the federal government has for responding to nuke/rad incidents (not otherwise specified in [[NRF]])
		- Discus integration of the concept of operations with other elements of the NRF
		- provide guidelines for notification, coordination, and leadership of the federal activities
	- introduces terms:
		- [[Primary Authority]]
			- those federal agencies that own have custody of authorize regulate or are otherwise assigned responsibility for the nuke/rad material facility or activity involved in an incident
		- Cooperating Agency
			- Which include other fed agencies that provide additional technical and resource support to specific nuke/rad incidents to the Dept of Homeland Sec ([[DHS]]) and the coordinating agencies
	- Scope:
		- Classification of various nuke/rad (N/R) events:
			- Inadvertant (accidental ) releases
				- From comercial or weapons production facilities
				- Lost rad material sources
				- transport accidents involving n/r material
				- domestic nuclear weapons incidents
				- foreign accidents involving n/r material that impact the US
			- releases related to deliberate acts
				- radiological diserpas devices (RDDs)
				- Improvised nuclear devices (INDs) (low quality nuclear weapon) 
				- Nuclear Weapons
		- Most common NR incidents consist of loss theft or mismanagement of relatively small rad mat sources mainly handled at local level
