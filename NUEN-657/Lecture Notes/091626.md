# Nuclear/Radiological Incident Annex ([[NRIA]]) to the [[FIOP|Response and Recovery Federal Interagency Operational Manual]]

Federal Support Determination
	- Nuclear/Radiological Incident Annex ([[NRIA]]) to the [[FIOP|Response and Recovery Federal Interagency Operational Manual]]
	- No Federal assistance in response
	- Federal Onsite Commander ([[OSC]]) determines no assistance is required acts as liaison in response
	- [[Unified Command]] response (UCR) determine
		- [[LFA]]
		- the supporting Agencies
		- [[ESF|Emergency Support functions]] needed
		- [[Stafford Act]] Response
			- President issues a major disaster declaration
			- Federal coordinating officer ([[FCO]]) is designated
			- joint field office is established ([[JFO]])
			- all ESFs are available
## Stafford Act activation
Activating Key Assets
	- The owner/operator of a N/R facility or materials has first responsibility for mitigating consequences of an incident
		- Notifies [[SLTT]] governments as well as relevant federal D/As
		- [[LFA]] determined
		- LFA notifies [[DHS]] National operations center ([[NOC]]) and other federal D/As
	- Once Notified LFA coordinates interagency response
N/R Incident Response Activities
	- Numerous response activities by various federal, state, tribal, and local entities are required during an N/R incident (Expertise required)
		- Incident security
		- Unknown material identification
		- Atmospheric plume modeling
		- Environmental monitoring and sampling for characterization and re-entry
		- Emergency worker monitoring
		- Protective action recommendations
		- Population monitoring
		- Laboratory analysis
		- Environmental monitoring and sampling for cleanup verification
		- Release of public information
		- Population decontamination
		- Emergency worker decontamination
		- Response equipment decontamination
		- Fatality management
		- Contaminated animal management
		- Contaminated agricultural product management
		- Radioactive waste storage and disposal
		- Contaminated debris removal
		- Environmental remediation
Key Elements of Response and Recovery
	- The agency with [[Primary Authority|Primary authority]] may organize the response, but it is DOE that manages radiological monitoring on the ground and on the air. (FRMAC)
	- DOE relinquishes FRMAC control to EPA for activities only after
		- Emergency is stabilized
		- Offsite releases have stopped
		- Off site conditions have been evaluated
		- initial long range monitoring plan has been developed with SLTT and other federal agencies
		- EPA has received assurance from other fed agencies they will support
	- Radiological monitoring and assessment activities are terminated when
		- there is no more threat to public safety or the environment
		- SLTTs are adequate for the situation
		- Mutual agreement between all parties to terminate

# United States Response Assets

	- Few US Federal Agencies have dedicated radiation response personnel
		- [[DOE]] (All Nuclear Emergency Support Team ([[NEST]]) programs)
			- Radiological Assistance Program ([[RAP]])
			- Consequence Management Response Team ([[CMRT]])
			- Aerial Measurement System ([[AMS]])
			- Radiation Emergency Assistance Center / Training Site ([[REACTS]])
			- National Atmospheric Release Capability ([[NARC]])
		- US [[EPA]]
			- National Decontamination Team
				- Radiological Emergency Response Team ([[RERT]])
		- Other Fed Agencies that can provide limited support
			- [[DOD]]:
				- Army
				- Navy
					- Navy treats it as a Co-60 release on a sub
					- Wanted to evacuate all 50k Navy personnel from Japan during [[Fukushima]]
			- Civil Support Teams ([[CST]]) State/National Guard assets
			- Nuclear Regulatory Commission ([[NRC]])
			- Health and Human Services
				- Center for Disease Control ([[CDC]])
					- Evacuation Facilities
				- Food and Drug Administration ([[FDA]])
					- Use of Potassium Iodide, prussian blue
				- Dept of Agriculture
				- [[FEMA]]
					- Logistics, ie [[CBRN Responder]]
