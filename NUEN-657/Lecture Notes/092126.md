# United States Response Assets
Dedicated Response Teams
	- Few US Federal Agencies have dedicated radiation response personnel
		- [[DOE]] (All Nuclear Emergency Support Team ([[NEST]]) programs)
			- Radiological Assistance Program ([[RAP]])
			- Consequence Management Response Team ([[CMRT]])
			- Aerial Measurement System ([[AMS]])
			- Radiation Emergency Assistance Center / Training Site ([[REACTS]])
			- National Atmospheric Release Capability ([[NARC]])
		- US [[EPA]]
			- National Decontamination Team
				- Radiological Emergency Response Team ([[RERT]])
		- Other Fed Agencies that can provide limited support
			- [[DOD]]:
				- Army
				- Navy
					- Navy treats it as a Co-60 release on a sub
					- Wanted to evacuate all 50k Navy personnel from Japan during [[Fukushima]]
			- Civil Support Teams ([[CST]]) State/National Guard assets
			- Nuclear Regulatory Commission ([[NRC]])
			- Health and Human Services
				- Center for Disease Control ([[CDC]])
					- Evacuation Facilities
				- Food and Drug Administration ([[FDA]])
					- Use of Potassium Iodide, prussian blue
				- Dept of Agriculture
				- [[FEMA]]
					- Logistics, ie [[CBRN Responder]]
For a Large Emergency: 1 voice
	- Federal Radiological Monitoring and Assessment Center ([[FRMAC]])
		- Should always be supporting state response not other way around
		- FED agency combined response
		- Managed/organized by DOE
		- 1 Voice Public Information Officer ([[PIO]])
			- Lessons learned from [[TMI]]
				- 5x fed agencies
				- 5x speakers to the public (now PIO)
				- 5x Operations center (now Joint Operations Center [[JOC]])
National Guard WMD, Civil Support Teams ([[CST]])
	- every state has one usually in state capital
		- (California has one for north one for south)
	- Regional and state focused
	- State Controlled (governor and adjutant general)
	- "Same" Equipment Instrumentation and Vehicles
EPA Assets
	- EPA has several specialized teams that will provide aid during a rad emergency
	- [[RERT]] Radiological response Team
		- Specialized unit that responds to emergencies requiring the cleanup of radioactive substances. RERT provides onsite and lab-based radiation risk monitoring services
			- [[RADNET]]
				- Fixed and Mobile radiation detection system![[Pasted image 20260921124657.png]]
	- Chemical, Biological, Radiological and Nuclear Consequence Management Advisory Division ([[CBRN CMAD]])
		- Provides scientific support and technical expertise for decontamination of:
			- buildings
			- building contents
			- public infrastructure
			- agriculture
			- associated environmental media
		- Also uses [[ASPECT]]
			- Airborne Real-time chem/rad detection
Radiological Assistance Program [[RAP]]
	- DOE
	- can deploy under their own authority without calling D.C.
	- next guys to show up after CST
	![[Pasted image 20260921125511.png]]
	- RAP Team
		- Support ranges from giving technical information or advice over telephone, to field support to accident sites to help identify and minimize any radiological hazards
		- 6-8 members
			- Fed Team lead
				- buffer between team and local pd, has overall authority
			- Captain
				- Directs how things are done in the field
			- Remainder Health Physics Personnel
		- Immediately deployable at field discretion
		- on scene within less than 8 hours after notification
Consequence Management Home Team ([[CMHT]])
	- Advisory group sits in room in Las Vegas or Sandia or has a bridge line between
	- acts in advisory role to people in the field
	- starts activating other assets
	- Bridge line people can call on and whole team can hear what is being said
	- Would activate the NARAC
National Atmospheric Release Advisory Center ([[NARAC]])
	- Livermore CA, scientist sleeps with laptop at their house and can start sims within 15 minutes
	- Computational systems for 3-D plume sims
	- uninterruptible, backup power
	- 24x7 scientific and technical support
	- Access to world wide weather data and geographical information
		- Observed & Forecasted weather data
		- Terrain & land surface
		- Maps
		- Population
	- Automated real-time 3-D plume model predictions for nuclear, radiological, chemical or biological releases available in minutes from national center using Internet/Web tool
	- Standalone simple plume modeling tools for end-user’s computer require no connection to NARAC
	- live feedback between field measurements and modeling
Aerial Measuring System ([[AMS]]) Fixed Wing
	- OLD PLANE: Beechcraft King Air B-200
		- Twin-engine turbo prop
		- IFR (all weather) rated
		- 260 knots (300 mph)
		- range 1,130 nm (1,300 sm)
		- Max endurance 5 hrs (without refueling)
	- Four person Crew
		- Pilot and co-pilot
		- Scientist/Health physicist
		- Technician/Technologist
	- Equipment
		- Spectral Aerial Radiological Computer System ([[SPARCS]])
	- AMS Rotary Wing, Lower and Slower
		- Bell 412
			- Twin-Pac turbo shaft engine
			- IFR (all weather) rated
			- 120 knots (140 mph)
			- Range 360 nm (410 sm)
			- Max Endurance 3 hrs (without refueling)
		- Three person crew
			- Pilot & copilot
			- Technician/Technologist
		- ground Support
			- Scientist / health physicist
			- Data Analyst
			- Aircraft Mechanic
AMS Mission Parameters
	- On Call Response Phase
		- Large Area (>25 mi2)
			- Altitude: 500-1,000 feet
			- Spacing: 1-5 miles
			- Speed: 140 knots
			- Fixed-wing Aircraft
	- Radiological Mapping Phase
		- Rapid Overview – Emergency Response
			- Altitude: 500 feet
			- Spacing: 0.5 mile
			- Speed: 80 knots
			- Helicopter
		- Small Area (<25 mi2) – Detailed Survey
			- Altitude: 150-300 feet
			- Line Spacing: 250-500 feet
			- Speed: 70 knots
Radiation Emergency Assistance Center/Training Site [[REACTS|REAC/TS]]
	- Might be part of home team especially at the very beginning
	- REAC/TS staff are available 24 hours a day/seven days a week to deploy and provide emergency medical consultation for incidents involving radiation anywhere in the world
	- Provide Biodosimetry
	- Provide training to medical personnel
