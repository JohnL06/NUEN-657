Terrestrial Dispersion
	Energy Flow and Nutrient Transfer
		- Radiant Energy (Sun) 2 $\dfrac{\text{calories}}{\text{cm}^2 \cdot \text{min}}$
		- Producers (Plants) 
			- convert sun energy to forms suitable for primary consumers and decomposers
				- convert energy to forms suitable for secondary consumers and decomposers
					- decomposers return nutrients to nutrient pool for producers
		- consumers eat everything
		-  nutrient pool is essential minerals and chem compounds for life
		- decomposers feed on and break down dead organic material
Soil Properties
	- Soils consist of the following arranged in a complicated physio-chemical system
	- Contents
		- Minerals
		- Organic matter
		- water
		- Air
	- Acts as foothold and supply nutrients for plants
	- Soil have a profile broken down into 3 major horizons
		- Top soil
			- First 30-60 cm 
			- area where most life processes take place
		- Sub Soil
			- up to 1 m below
		- Parent Material
			- 1.5 m
			- loose and partly decayed rock
Inorganic Portion of surface soils
	- Soils have a number of textural classes
		- Sand
			- Primary minerals such as quartz
			- Particle sizes 60 $\mu$m - 2mm
		- Silts
			- Particle sizes 2 - 60 $\mu$m
		- Clays
			- Particles smaller than 2 $\mu$m

Ion Exchange
	- Most nutrient ions are not dissolved in water, but are absorbed on the surfaces of the soil particles
		- A greater reservoir of nutrient elements can be held in this way than in solution
		- Nutrients would be rapidly leached from soils if not for clays abilities to bind elements
			- Estimated that 1$m^3$ of Loam has a surface area of 2 million $m^2$ 
			- Cations (+ ions) in water solution are exchanged with exchanged with cations sorbed to the **surface** of the clays
Transport in Soil
	- soil surface 1-3 mm
		- up into atmosphere from soil is resuspension 
		- down to root zone via Percolation (Combination of physical and chemical processes to redistribute surface radionuclides to lower levels via animal movement, tilling/digging, and rain wash)
	- Root zone
		- up into soil surface through heaving (i.e. hoe or cracks in dry soil)
		- fixed to soil, K_d of soil describes how readily they can be transferred between soils and depth
		- down into deeper soil via leaching/percolation
	- Deeper Soil
Movement with liquids
	- When water moves, sorption of ions occurs at different rates depending on the physiochemical properties of the ions and media
		- Different ionic species sorb at different rates
		- most weakly bound species are at the leading edge
		- Cations are generally more strongly sorbed than anions (-)
			- more negative ions in soil
Solid vs liquid phase
	- It is important to understand how the radionuclide is partitioned between solid an liquid phase.
		- If primarily in liquid phase -> movement
	- K_d is the distribution of ??? coef
		- K_d = $\dfrac{\text{soild phse}}{\text{Liquid Phase}}$ = ml / g
		- High K_d implies little leaching, little movement
		- Low K_d more leaching ispossible, fast movement
		- Smaller multivalent ions usually exhibit larger Kd than univalent ions
		- Fraction sorbed is typically inversally proportional to particle size

In the air
	- Particulates can be inhaled
		- particulates on the ground can be pushed back into the air
	- different types of surface transport
		- resuspension (particles less than 50 $\mu$m)
		- Saltation (50-100 $\mu$m), kinda jumping up and falling back down
		- Surface creep (larger than 100 $\mu$m)
		- Particles are always in the air
			- mass loading (X)
				- $X (\dfrac{Bq}{m^3}) = M_\text{soil in air} \dfrac{G}{m^3} \cdot M_\text{surface soil} \dfrac{Bq}{g})$
				- M can range from 10^-6 in pristine to 10^-2 in windy/dry areas
Resuspension
	- process by which contaminants are removed from surface and put back in air
	- Respension factor
		- $F_r \left( \frac{1}{M} \right) = \frac{X \, \left( \frac{\text{Bq}}{\text{m}^3} \right)}{S \, \left( \frac{\text{Bq}}{\text{m}^2} \right)}$
	-  Air samplers are deployed to measure resuspension so we can determine (air)
		- X = F * S
		- Estimate (air) based on F_r -> limatations, not exact values
