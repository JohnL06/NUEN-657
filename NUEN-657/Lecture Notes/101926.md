# [[EPA400]]: Protective Action Guides
Built from the lecture transcript and slides — sources: [[101926 EPA400 transcript.pdf|transcript]], [[Lecture 9 EPA 400.pptx|slides]], [[EPA_PAG_Manual_2017.pdf|EPA-400 (2017)]], [[PAG_reference.pdf|responder reference values]]
Reading: chapters 3, 4 and 5 of EPA-400 (in this week's module), plus the Fukushima evacuation article (likely a quiz question)
	- nursing home moved and kept on a bus 48+ hours without food or water, 6-7 died of dehydration; cancer risk from the radiation for that population was essentially nil

Definitions
	- [[PAG]] - Protective Action Guide: dose guideline at which protective actions are warranted; projected dose accumulated over time
		- guidelines not laws — very few laws on when to evacuate/shelter/relocate, so there is room to think
	- [[PAD]] - Protective Action Decision: what the decision maker actually decides, usually based on a PAG
	- [[DRL]] - Derived Response Level: a measurable value projected to meet a PAG
		- ex: send field teams out to find 5 mrem/h, because 5 mrem/h over ~100 h hits the 1 rem evacuation PAG
	- [[DIL]] - Derived Intervention Level: measurable amount in food/liquid that if ingested could meet a PAG -> embargo the food
		- "intervening" between the food and the people
	- DCRL came up in a student question — prof thought it is a [[FRMAC manual]] term, roughly the same idea applied to something else

History
	- EPA tasked with creating radiological emergency guidelines
	- EPA 1992 ("EPA 400") used for 20+ years, new versions 2013 and 2017 (current)
	- 1992 used ICRP 26/30 for dose projections, now ICRP 60
	- Legal basis: 44 CFR 351 & Executive Order 12656
	- Kept the 3 phases but consolidated action plans
	- New PAGs have input from FRPCC, DOE, DOD, FEMA, NRC, HHS, CDC, FDA, USDA, DOL (1992 relied on EPA's own expertise)
	- Scope expanded
		- 1992 was based only on nuclear power plant releases (fission products) — post [[TMI]]/Chernobyl thinking
		- now: NPPs, fuel cycle facilities, weapons facilities, transportation accidents, radiopharmaceuticals, launch vehicles, [[RDD]]s, INDs
		- driven by real responses (transport, fuel facilities, criticality accidents) and [[9.11]]/terrorism concern
		- downside: no longer a fixed set of fission products, have to estimate what you are worried about
	- PAGs are implemented using [[FRMAC manual]] calculations and methods
		- FRMAC manual is multi-agency, not a federal guideline document, so it updates much faster
		- EPA-400 itself says: review the current FRMAC Assessment Manual for the most current methods; organ dose thresholds for evacuation and sheltering were removed
		- matters because states used to insist on following 1992 EPA-400 over newer FRMAC methods — now you can point to this statement, but you are there to support, not dictate
		- Turbo FRMAC can run earlier dose model versions if a state insists
	- Changes from 1992
		- FDA 1998 food interdiction guidance integrated
		- KI PAG lowered to 5 rem (child thyroid)
		- removed the intermediate-phase relocation PAG of 5 rem over 50 years (too hard to calculate, too much conservatism)
		- all other PAGs stayed the same

[[Response Phases]]
	- Early (emergency) phase - hours to days
		- PAGs may be based on default procedures, e.g. site area emergency -> evacuate 2 miles in all directions, no calculations
		- ex: San Luis Obispo bus drivers issued KI for Diablo Canyon
	- Intermediate phase - weeks to months
		- source and releases brought under control (not necessarily)
		- [[Fukushima]] was a "never-ending early phase" - intermediate maybe a month in
	- Late phase - months to years
		- begins when actions can reduce radiation to acceptable levels (acceptable to the scientist ≠ to the decision maker)
		- PAG levels / dose to avoid not appropriate here, which is why the 50-year PAG was dropped

Phase 1: Emergency Phase
	- Exposure pathways: inhalation, deposition, direct exposure (plume submersion); ingestion minor this early
	- Define exposure areas from environmental measurements and model estimates of the source term
		- [[NARAC]] does the modeling; for an NPP, [[NRC]] knows the burnup and configuration for the source term
		- [[RASCAL]] paperwork needs physical signatures; Turbo FRMAC also available
	- PAGs - principal actions: evacuation, [[shelter in place]] (SIP), and administration of [[Potassium Iodide]]
		- SIP/evacuation: 1 to 5 rem projected over 4 days (96 h, sometimes said 100 h)
			- start at 1 rem; don't try to anticipate going over
		- KI: 5 rem projected child thyroid dose (infant/toddler most sensitive)
	- [[evacuation]]
		- most effective if completed before plume passage
		- may increase exposure if done incorrectly - traffic jams under the plume (Texas hurricane evacuations)
		- may only be effective close to the source; evacuating 10-20 miles out may not be smart
		- TMI: the only death associated was a child who drowned at the family's vacation home after evacuating
		- people self-evacuate anyway (Manhattan after 9/11)
		- may not be plausible due to weather or road conditions
		- NEED TO TAKE INTO ACCOUNT EMERGENCY RESPONDER CAPABILITY IF RECOMMENDING EVACUATION
	- [[shelter in place]]
		- comes with additional instructions: HVAC off, tape windows/gaps, most interior room
		- can be implemented rapidly
		- buildings and cars have shielding coefficients (dose reduction factors)
			- 1-story home ~2-3x, basement ~10x (an order of magnitude)
			- multi-story: go low; middle floors of large buildings best; large structures 100-200x
			- dose mostly from deposition on the roof plus groundshine
			- car: small reduction, but better than outside
	- [[Potassium Iodide]] (KI)
		- protects the thyroid only, only against radioiodine — useless against everything else
		- most effective before exposure, still effective up to ~2 hours after
		- may require approval of state medical officials
		- FDA thresholds (thyroid dose): ages 0-18 ≥5 rem; adults 18-40 and pregnant/lactating ≥10 rem; over 40 ≥500 rem
		- prof warned about shellfish allergy and medical advice — NOTE the 2017 manual (p.21) says iodine and shellfish allergies are not related
	- Response workers ([[emergency limits]])
		- maintain ALARA; if you are not getting dose you probably aren't working hard enough
		- 5-10-25: 5 rem all occupational, 10 rem protecting critical infrastructure (e.g. a power plant), 25 rem lifesaving / protection of large populations
		- >25 rem only for volunteers fully aware of the risks; NCRP 165 decision dose up to 50 rem with signed acknowledgement (~4% increase in cancer risk at most)
		- "time-sensitive, mission critical" activities - [[NCRP 165]]
		- also follow OSHA 29 CFR, EPA 40 CFR, NRC 10 CFR 20, DOE 10 CFR 835

Phase 2: Intermediate Phase
	- Used when source and releases are under control and environmental measurements are available
	- Exposure pathways
		- whole body from groundshine dominates (NPP, RDD, IND)
		- inhalation from [[Resuspension]] (working the area, wind, rain)
		- ingestion of food and water
	- Principal protective actions: [[relocation]], decontamination, shielding, time limits, controlling spread of contamination
		- spreading vegetable oil to fix contamination got pushback from EPA as pollution
		- relocation is most effective but highly disruptive
	- PAGs
		- relocation: >2 rem projected in the first year; 0.5 rem/year in the second and subsequent years ("second year PAG")
		- simple dose reduction techniques: <2 rem first year
		- food interdiction: 0.5 rem/year whole body or 5 rem/year to any organ
		- drinking water: 100 mrem/yr sensitive populations, 500 mrem/yr general population
		- reentry: operational guidelines
	- Relocation areas refined by environmental factors (plume direction) — "keyhole": circle around the site plus downwind out to 5/10/25 miles
	- Relocation from phases 1 and 2 creates 3 groups
		- already evacuated
		- not previously evacuated, but should be
		- evacuated but outside the relocation area, so may return home
	- Priorities (roughly in order)
		- determine where the first-year PAG may be exceeded
		- verify clear areas with field teams; let evacuated populations and buffer zones return toward the PAG contour
		- minimize relocation
		- evaluate simple decon techniques
		- control access to relocation areas (with reasonable exceptions — pets, valuables, dairy cows)
		- set up monitoring and decon stations (with EPA and the National Decontamination Team)
		- collect data for long-term criteria
		- evaluate produce and animal products in the plume area

Phase 3: Late Phase
	- Long-term cleanup, for nationally significant incidents (NPP, RDD, IND)
	- PAGs will NOT be used to guide restoration
	- Balance restoring business, reducing radiation risk, radioactive decay, and managing contaminated waste
	- [[FRMAC]] in charge of monitoring during the emergency, then switches over to [[EPA]] for restoration/cleanup
