## 1. Who to call, and in what order
1. **Owner/operator** of the N/R facility or material has first responsibility → notifies [[SLTT]] governments + relevant federal D/As
2. **[[LFA]] determined** — the agency with [[Primary Authority]] (owns, has custody of, authorizes, regulates, or is assigned the facility/activity). e.g. [[NRC]] for a licensed NPP, [[DOE]] for its own sites
3. LFA notifies the **[[DHS]] [[NOC]]** and other federal D/As; LFA then coordinates the interagency response
4. **Federal support determination** ([[NRIA]]):
	- no federal assistance, or
	- federal [[OSC]] decides none is needed and acts as liaison, or
	- **[[Unified Command]] response** decides the LFA, supporting agencies, [[ESF]]s needed, and whether a [[Stafford Act]] response applies
5. **[[Stafford Act]] response:** President issues a major disaster declaration → [[FCO]] designated → [[JFO]] established → all ESFs available
6. **Protective action decision maker** = usually the governor; **in Texas, the county judge**. Scientists *advise* — "I can't tell you that, these are the guidelines"

**Lead-up timeline:** atmospheric sims running 15-20 min after an event, first results in 30-60 min · [[NARAC]] can start sims within 15 min · [[RAP]] on scene < 8 h

## 2. Which teams — who does what
| Team | Parent | What it does | Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[CST]] | State / National Guard | Detect & ID unknown CBRN material, advise | One per state, usually the capital (CA has 2); **governor + adjutant general** control; usually **first** |
| [[RAP]] | [[DOE]] ([[NEST]]) | First federal rad asset; phone advice → field support | **Deploys on own authority without calling D.C.**; on scene **< 8 h**; 6-8 people (Fed Team Lead, Captain, HPs); next after CST |
| [[CMHT]] | DOE | Reach-back advisory home team | Las Vegas / Sandia + open **bridge line**; scalable, near-immediate; **activates NARAC**; receives data |

| [[CMRT]] | DOE ([[NEST]]) | Full field support, nucleus of FRMAC | **50-100 people** |

| [[A-TEAM]] | [[EPA]], [[FDA]], [[CDC]], [[USDA]] | Turns FRMAC data into **recommendations** | PAGs, interpreting regs, modeling assumptions |
| [[AMS]] | DOE ([[NEST]]) | Aerial radiation mapping | Fixed wing (King Air 350ER, old B-200) + helicopter (Bell 412); large area >25 mi2 at 500-1,000 ft; detailed <25 mi2 at 150-300 ft |

| [[RERT]] | [[EPA]] | Cleanup + onsite/lab monitoring | Uses [[RADNET]] (fixed & mobile detection) |
| [[CBRN CMAD]] | EPA | Decon science | Buildings, contents, infrastructure, ag; runs [[ASPECT]] (airborne chem/rad) |
| [[NEST]] | DOE / [[NNSA]] | Umbrella | RAP, CMRT, AMS, NARAC, REAC/TS |
| [[CDC]] / [[FDA]] / [[USDA]] | HHS / HHS / Ag | Public health / KI, Prussian blue, food DILs / agriculture | |
| [[FEMA]] | DHS | Logistics | [[CBRN Responder]] |
| [[DOD]] | | Limited support | Navy treats a release like Co-60 on a sub |

**Send data to FRMAC:** [[RadResponder]] → or email/fax the CMHT → or call in on the CMHT bridge line
**Who pays for FRMAC:** NOT the state/local/tribal jurisdiction — [[DHS]] generally absorbs it
**FRMAC at the [[EOC]]:** 1 liaison and/or 1 scientific advisor
**FRMAC site:** 10,000-20,000 sq ft near highways/airports with power + parking (schools, hockey rinks, farm centers, armories)

**FRMAC → [[EPA]] handover only after:** emergency stabilized · offsite releases stopped · offsite conditions evaluated · initial long-range monitoring plan with SLTT + feds · EPA has assurance of federal support
**Monitoring ends when:** no more threat to public/environment · SLTT capabilities adequate · all parties agree

## 3. PAGs — EPA-400 (2017)
[[EPA_PAG_Manual_2017.pdf#page=16|Table 1-1, p.6 — full summary table]]

| Phase | Action | PAG |
|---|---|---|
| Early | [[shelter in place]] / [[evacuation]] | **1-5 rem projected over 4 days** (96 h); start at **1 rem** |
| Early | [[Potassium Iodide]] | **5 rem projected child thyroid dose** |
| Early & Int. | Emergency workers | **5 rem** (higher under exceptional circumstances — see §5) |
| Intermediate | [[relocation]] | **> 2 rem first year**; **0.5 rem/yr** second & later years |
| Intermediate | Simple dose reduction | < 2 rem first year |
| Intermediate | Food interdiction | **0.5 rem/yr whole body** or **5 rem/yr any organ** |
| Intermediate | Drinking water | **100 mrem/yr** sensitive pops (infants, children, pregnant, nursing); **500 mrem/yr** general |
| Intermediate | Reentry | Operational guidelines (stay times) — [[EPA_PAG_Manual_2017.pdf#page=60|Table 4-2, p.50]] |
| Late | Cleanup / waste | **No PAG** — planning guidance only |

Projected dose = external (groundshine + plume submersion) + committed effective dose from inhalation
**Removed:** the old 5 rem over 50 years relocation PAG

Definitions: [[PAG]] = projected dose at which an action is warranted · [[PAD]] = the decision actually made · [[DRL]] = measurable environmental level that would produce the PAG (e.g. 5 mrem/h × ~100 h ≈ 1 rem) · [[DIL]] = measurable food/liquid concentration → embargo

## 4. Potassium iodide — [[EPA_PAG_Manual_2017.pdf#page=31|Table 2-2, p.21]]
| Group | Projected thyroid dose to give KI | KI dose |
|---|---|---|
| Adults > 40 | ≥ 500 rem | 130 mg |
| Adults 18-40 | ≥ 10 rem | 130 mg |
| Pregnant / lactating | ≥ 10 rem | 130 mg |
| 12-18 (adult-sized >150 lb → 130 mg) | ≥ 5 rem | 65 mg |
| 3-12 years | ≥ 5 rem | 65 mg |
| 1 month-3 years | ≥ 5 rem | 32 mg |
| Birth-1 month | ≥ 5 rem | 16 mg |

Thyroid only, radioiodine only · best before exposure, still helps up to ~2 h after · may need state medical officials' approval · 1-year-olds get the largest thyroid dose

## 5. Emergency workers — [[EPA_PAG_Manual_2017.pdf#page=45|Table 3-1, p.35]] · [[PAG_reference.pdf|reference values sheet]]
| Dose | Activity |
|---|---|
| **5 rem** | All occupational exposures |
| **10 rem** | Protecting critical infrastructure (e.g. a power plant) |
| **25 rem** | Lifesaving / protecting large populations |
| **> 25 rem** | Same, only for people fully aware of the risks |
| 50 rad | NCRP 165 **decision dose** — remove responder or continue |
| 50 rem / 100 rem | IAEA **turn-back**: prevent severe effects / lifesaving |

| Zone | Rate | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Cold zone (outer perimeter) | ≤ 10 mR/h | Alarm threshold |
| Hot zone | > 10 mR/h | Routine work with PPE + active monitoring |
| Dangerous-radiation zone | ≥ 10 R/h | Time-sensitive, mission-critical only |
| Turn back | 200 R/h | Turn back even on lifesaving missions |

NRIA admin limits (09/14): 10 rem for protection of property · 10 rem over 48 h (12 h workdays) in the 96 h emergency
Also follow: OSHA 29 CFR · EPA 40 CFR · NRC 10 CFR 20 · DOE 10 CFR 835 · ALARA always · [[NCRP 165]]

**Acute radiation syndrome** ([[EPA_PAG_Manual_2017.pdf#page=46|Table 3-2, p.36]]): 0-100 rad no vomiting, no mortality · 100-200 rad 5-50% vomit at 3-6 h, minimal mortality · 200-600 rad 50-100% vomit at 2-4 h, low mortality with aggressive therapy · 600-800 rad 75-100% vomit at 1-2 h, high mortality · > 800 rad 90-100% vomit in < 1 h, very high mortality

## 6. Protective action details
**Evacuation:** best if done before plume passage · can *increase* dose (traffic jams under the plume) · may only make sense close to the source · **account for emergency responder capability** · TMI's only associated death was a drowning after evacuating; Fukushima nursing-home deaths from a bad evacuation
**Shelter in place:** fast to implement; HVAC off, seal gaps, interior room · dose reduction: 1-story home ~2-3×, **basement ~10×**, large multi-story 100-200×, go low/middle floors, car only slightly better · [[EPA_PAG_Manual_2017.pdf#page=29|Figure 2-1, p.19]]
**Relocation:** "keyhole" — circle around the site + downwind wedge to 5/10/25 mi · 3 groups: already evacuated / should be relocated / evacuated but outside → may return

| | Early | Intermediate | Late |
|---|---|---|---|
| Duration | hours-days | weeks-months | months-years |
| Basis | defaults (e.g. evacuate 2 mi all directions) | environmental measurements | cleanup planning |
| Pathways | inhalation, deposition, direct/plume | **groundshine dominates**, resuspension, ingestion | — |
| Actions | evacuate, SIP, KI | relocation, decon, shielding, time limits, food/water | no PAGs; restoration, waste |

## 8. Formulas & units
| | |
|---|---|
| Exposure | 1 R = **2.58e-4 C/kg** air · 1 C/kg = 3876 R |
| R → rad (air) | 1 R ≈ 8.73e-3 Gy = **0.873 rad** (W = 34 eV/ion pair) |
| Units | 1 Gy = 100 rad · 1 Sv = 100 rem · 1 rem = 10 mSv · 1 Ci = 3.7e10 Bq |
| Equivalent dose | H = D · w_R (γ, β = 1; α = 20) |
| Exposure rate | Ẋ = Γ·A / d² |
| Rule of thumb (m) | Ẋ (R/h) ≈ **0.5 · Σ(F·E) · A(Ci) / d(m)²** — E in MeV, F = yield |
| Rule of thumb (ft) | Ẋ (R/h) ≈ **6 · Σ(F·E) · A(Ci) / d(ft)²** |
| Gaussian plume (ground level) | χ(x,y) = Q / (π σy σz ū) · exp(−y²/2σy²) · exp(−h²/2σz²) |
| Max downwind | χ_max = Q / (e π ū σy σz), where σz = h/√2; max ground conc ∝ 1/h² |
| Plume validity | flat terrain: within a factor of 2-3 out to 10 km |
| [[Kd]] | solid / liquid (mL/g) — high = stays put, low = moves |
| [[Concentration Ratio]] | CR = veg / soil · Sr >> I >> Ba > Cs |
| [[Resuspension factor]] | Fr (1/m) = X (Bq/m³) / S (Bq/m²) → X = Fr · S |
| [[Mass loading]] | X (Bq/m³) = M (g/m³) × soil activity (Bq/g); M ~1e-6 pristine to 1e-2 windy/dry |
| [[Weathering]] | veg = veg₀ · e^(−(kw + λ)t), kw = ln2/Tw, Tw ≈ 14 d (≤ 20 μm), 10 d for I-131 |
| [[Effective half-life]] | 1/Te = 1/Tp + 1/Tb |
Dose types: effective (organ weighted) · CED/CEDE (committed, from an intake) · **TEDE = external + committed internal**
Particle transport: [[Resuspension]] < 50 μm · [[Saltation]] 50-100 μm · [[Surface creep]] > 100 μm

## 10. Laws & frameworks timeline
1979 [[FEMA]] · 1988 [[Stafford Act]] / [[FRP]] authorized · 1992 FRP (augmented by [[NCP]] 1968 + CERCLA 1980) · 1992 [[EPA400]] · 1996 [[FRERP]] (NRC + FEMA) · 2003 [[HPSD-5]] → [[NIMS]] · 2004 [[NRP]] · 2008 [[NRF]] (rev. 2013, 2019) · EPA-400 revised 2013 & **2017** (legal basis 44 CFR 351 + EO 12656; ICRP 26/30 → ICRP 60)
NRF principles: engaged partnership · tiered response · scalable/flexible/adaptable · unity of effort through unified command · readiness to act
[[NPG]]: 5 mission areas (prevention, protection, mitigation, response, recovery) · 32 core capabilities
[[ICS]]: [[Incident Commander]] always staffed · **Unity of Command** = one supervisor each · **[[Unified Command]]** = agencies jointly, no single commander · **Chain of Command** = orderly line of authority

## 12. Watch out — lecture vs. the manual / errors in notes
- **KI & shellfish:** lecture warned against it; **EPA-400 p.21 says iodine and shellfish allergies are not related**
- **Worker 5 rem:** lecture called it per event, not annual; Table 1-1 lists it as 5 rem/year, total over the entire response
- **1 R = 2.58e-4 C/kg** (the 10/12 note has 2.88e-4 — a typo)
- **Gaussian plume exponent is negative** (the 10/14 note is missing the minus sign)
- **Weathering with decay:** e^(−(kw + λ)t), not e^(−kw + λt) (10/07 note)
- EPA-400 says to use the **current FRMAC Assessment Manual** methods; organ dose thresholds for evacuation/sheltering were removed
